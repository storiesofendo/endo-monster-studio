# Book style: "There's an Endo-Monster in My Tummy!"

Edit this file so it matches the book. It is added to every image prompt and
to the script-writer's instructions.

## Look
- Soft hand-painted children's picture-book illustration, watercolor and gouache texture.
- Gentle rounded shapes, warm pastel palette (rose pinks, soft teal, butter yellow), clean dark outlines on characters.
- Backgrounds are painterly and slightly less saturated than the characters so the characters pop.
- Friendly, cozy, never scary. Suitable for ages 4-9.

## Voice and story rules
- Short, simple sentences a young child understands. One idea per line.
- Pain is real and is taken seriously by grown-ups; the Endo-Monster is silly-grumpy, not frightening.
- No medical advice beyond "tell a grown-up you trust" and "doctors can help".
- Every episode ends hopeful.
- Each episode brings a NEW guest friend, an "Endo Warrior" (FRIEND), who shares one brave trick for handling endo.
- The Endo-Monster is sneaky and mischievous: he sneaks in (`@ENDO-MONSTER sneak left`), peeks around the edge
  of the picture (`@ENDO-MONSTER peek right`), causes a tummy twist, and is foiled when Petunia speaks up and uses
  the brave trick. He is a recurring trouble-maker, never truly scary.
- Use motion lines to keep it lively: `@NAME enter left`, `@NAME exit right`, `@NAME hop`, `@NAME shake`.
