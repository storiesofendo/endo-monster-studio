# =========================== PART 2: IS IT MY FAULT? ===========================

SCENE bedroom: PETUNIA left, ENDO-MONSTER farright
NARRATOR: That night, Petunia couldn't sleep. She read her favorite book, but the words kept swimming around.

# =========================== PART 3: I HEAR YOU ===========================

SCENE doctor: MOM farleft, PETUNIA left, DR-SALEM right
@DR-SALEM enter right
DR-SALEM [happy]: Hello, Petunia. I'm Doctor Salem. I hear you've been having some very big tummy days.
PETUNIA [worried]: Really big ones. They twist and pinch, and sometimes I can't go to school.
DR-SALEM [neutral]: Thank you for telling me. I hear you. And I believe you.
PETUNIA [surprised]: You believe me? Just like that?
DR-SALEM [happy]: Just like that. Can you point to where it hurts the most?
PETUNIA [hurting]: Right here. Down low. And sometimes it goes all the way to my back.
DR-SALEM [neutral]: I want to show you something. This is one of my favorite picture books.
NARRATOR: Doctor Salem opened a big book. Inside was a drawing of a tummy, with bright little patches scattered around.
DR-SALEM [neutral]: Sometimes, little patches grow in places inside the body where they don't belong. Doctors call it endometriosis.
PETUNIA [surprised]: En-do-me-tri-o-sis. That's a really long word.
DR-SALEM [happy]: It is! So lots of people just call it endo. Those patches can get sore and swollen, and that's what makes the twisty feeling.
PETUNIA [surprised]: So it's not my fault?
DR-SALEM [neutral]: Not even a little. You didn't eat the wrong thing or jump in the wrong puddle. It's just something some bodies have.
PETUNIA [neutral]: So my monster has a name. Endo. The Endo-Monster.
DR-SALEM [happy]: I like that name. And here's the good news. There are lots of ways we can help keep that monster quiet.
DR-SALEM [neutral]: To find out exactly where those patches are hiding, I'd like to take a look with a tiny camera, during a little surgery.
PETUNIA [worried]: Surgery? Like... cutting?
DR-SALEM [neutral]: Just a few teeny tiny openings, smaller than your fingernail. You'll be fast asleep the whole time, and you won't feel a thing.
MOM [neutral]: And we'll be right there when you wake up.
PETUNIA [worried]: Can I think about it?
DR-SALEM [happy]: Of course. Brave doesn't mean not scared. Brave means asking all your questions.
